Everything you are given is under /app/data: a phased VCF for a sample of diploid individuals from one population, with the ancestral allele annotated at every site, and a piecewise-constant recombination map. /app/data/README_DATA.txt documents both file formats and lists the parameters you may treat as known, among them the mutation rate and the fitness convention that fixes what a selection coefficient means.

Two things about these data are not recoverable from the files themselves. The population passed through a bottleneck and later grew. And a number of sites in the genome have been targets of recent positive selection, but the selected sites are not in the VCF: a variant that reached fixation is monomorphic in the sample and has been filtered out, as have multiallelic sites. Recover the demographic history and the selected sites, and write both to /app/results.json.

Four terms are used below with exact meanings, and these are the definitions your answer is graded against. A sweep is complete if the beneficial allele reached a frequency of 0.98 or more in the population, and partial if it is still segregating below that frequency today. A sweep is soft if two or more independent origins of the beneficial allele, meaning separate mutations at the same site each arising on its own haplotype background, are still present in the population today, and hard if exactly one is. Crossing the two gives the four classes "hard_complete", "hard_partial", "soft_complete" and "soft_partial"; nothing in those labels refers to a sweep's age. The selection coefficient s is per-copy and additive, under the fitness convention stated in README_DATA.txt. The completion time of a complete sweep is the number of generations before the present at which the beneficial allele first reached frequency 0.98.

/app/results.json must contain a single JSON object with two keys. "demography" is an object whose five fields are all positive numbers: "N_ancestral", "N_bottleneck", "N_current", "T_bottleneck_start_gens_ago" and "T_bottleneck_end_gens_ago", the last two being the generations before the present at which the population size dropped to N_bottleneck and at which it rose from it. "sweeps" is a list carrying one object per selected site you report, each with "chrom", an integer chromosome number counting from 1; "position", a 1-based position in base pairs within that chromosome; "class", exactly one of the four names above; "selection_coefficient", a number strictly between 0 and 1; and "time_gens_ago", the completion time in generations, which is required for every sweep you report as hard_complete or soft_complete and is ignored, and may be omitted or null, for the two partial classes. A one-sweep example of the shape:

{"demography": {"N_ancestral": 10000, "N_bottleneck": 1200, "N_current": 9000, "T_bottleneck_start_gens_ago": 3400, "T_bottleneck_end_gens_ago": 3000}, "sweeps": [{"chrom": 2, "position": 4531000, "class": "soft_complete", "selection_coefficient": 0.03, "time_gens_ago": 180}]}

Every one of the following has to hold, and all of them are checked against the simulation's own record of what happened. On the history: N_ancestral and N_current must each be within a factor of 1.6 of the truth, and the quantity (T_bottleneck_start_gens_ago - T_bottleneck_end_gens_ago) / N_bottleneck within a factor of 2.5 of the truth. The depth and the duration of a bottleneck are only jointly identifiable from a single present-day sample and its absolute timing is not recoverable from these data at all, so neither is graded on its own, but the history you report must be a genuine contraction and recovery: N_bottleneck below both of the other two sizes, and the start of the bottleneck older than its end.

On finding the sweeps: reported sweeps closer together than 250 kb are collapsed into one before anything is graded, and a report counts as finding a true sweep if it is on the same chromosome and within 250 kb of it, each true sweep being found by at most one report and each report finding at most one true sweep. At least three of the true hard_complete sweeps, and at least one true sweep of each of the other three classes, must be found. At most one report may fail to match any true sweep; a second unmatched report fails the task, whether it sits in a recombination cold spot or anywhere else.

On characterizing them, over the reports that matched a true sweep: at least 70 percent must carry the right class. At least 70 percent of the reported selection coefficients must be within a factor of 2.5 of the truth, and the Spearman rank correlation between the reported and the true coefficients must be at least 0.50, so one coefficient repeated across your calls will not do; the true values span a factor of more than nine. Completion times are graded over the matched sweeps whose true class is hard_complete, using the true class rather than the one you reported, and at least three of them must be dated to within 50 percent of the true completion time or within 150 generations, whichever is the larger.
